How to Test Your Laptop Keyboard

A complete built-in keyboard diagnostic in four steps.

01
Click to Focus
Click anywhere on this page. Your browser needs window focus to capture key events from your laptop's built-in keyboard — close any fullscreen app first.
02
Press Every Key
Work row by row from Escape to the bottom row. Press each key and confirm it turns green. Pay special attention to keys you use most and any that have felt different recently.
03
Identify Faulty Keys
Click "Show Faulty" to highlight all unresponsive keys in gold. Note their positions — a cluster of adjacent failing keys suggests debris or liquid damage. Scattered failures point to driver issues.
04
Record the Results
Screenshot the test results before contacting support or a repair shop. The pattern of failed keys helps a technician diagnose the cause quickly and gives you evidence for warranty claims.

Laptop Keyboard Test — FAQ

Common questions from laptop users diagnosing keyboard problems.

If the built-in keyboard is completely unresponsive, connect a USB external keyboard and use it to open this page and run the test. Navigate to this page using the external keyboard, then switch to your laptop's built-in keyboard and press each key. Any key that lights up is partially working. If nothing lights up from the built-in keyboard at all, the issue is likely a disconnected ribbon cable or a failed keyboard controller — both require physical repair.

Act immediately. Power off the laptop, hold it upside down to drain liquid, and do not press any keys. Remove the battery if your laptop model allows it. Allow at least 48 hours to dry in a warm room — do not use a hairdryer. After drying, use this tester to map exactly which keys are affected. A cluster of failing keys in the path of the spill confirms liquid damage. Keys affected by sugary drinks may stick or become intermittent even after drying — isopropyl alcohol cleaning may be needed.

Windows updates can reset accessibility settings or update HID drivers in ways that affect keyboard behaviour. Try these steps in order: (1) Open Settings → Accessibility → Keyboard and turn off Filter Keys, Sticky Keys and Toggle Keys. (2) Open Device Manager, find your keyboard under "Keyboards", right-click and choose "Update driver". (3) If issues persist, right-click → "Uninstall device", restart the laptop, and Windows will reinstall the driver automatically. (4) Check Settings → Time & Language → Language to confirm the correct keyboard layout is still selected.
